{\small\bfseries\color{accent}110 OBSERVER}\\[7pt]
{\LARGE\bfseries Observability of a System's Own Past}\\[5pt]
{\large Message Cost, Causal Witnesses, and the Limits of Inference}\\[10pt]
Aleksandr Strand \hspace{1em} $\cdot$ \hspace{1em} 20 November 2026
\end{center}
\noindent\textbf{Abstract.} How much information about the past does a system need for its next decision, and how can we test whether it used that information? We give a criterion for exact decoding and determine the cost of the message. After configurations of the main Rule~110 lattice merge, an observer returns a sensor reading stored in an external one-bit register. At the next update the lattice recovers its earlier bit. In frozen OLMo, before a new string appears, the sender encodes its earlier choice and the feedback in a single rule token. The four combinations of choice and feedback produce four messages, which are reused across new strings. For strings with different end bits, the recipient solves 64/64 development conditions with the transmitted token and 32/64 with the constant token A. On the entire development panel it answers 124/128 conditions correctly and solves 6/8 complete blocks. An intervention on the channel tests rule transfer. An inference about subjective experience requires additional premises.
