\section{Underdetermination of experience and a conditional bridge}
\label{sec:boundary}
Retention of a bit establishes a property of a system under specified interventions. Can we infer from those observations that this particular system, at this particular time, has a subjective experience?

\paragraph{T5.} Suppose all axioms of the operational theory $T$ are formulated without the experience predicate $E(a,t)$. Take any model $M$ of this theory with a distinguished point $u=(a,t)$. Expand it first by interpreting $E$ as the empty set, then by interpreting $E$ as a set containing $u$. Since only the new predicate changes, both structures satisfy $T$ and agree on the outcomes of all operational interventions. In the first structure $E(u)$ is false, in the second it is true. Thus $T$ determines neither truth value, even when the functional criterion for revision holds. This is a result about the expressive power of the theory. Constructing two structures leaves open the physical realizability of two different experiences on one substrate.

\paragraph{T6.} The package $B=(B_{\mathrm{id}},B_{\mathrm{nom}},B_{\mathrm{epist}})$ has status \texttt{DECLARED\_NOT\_DERIVED}. Its three parts specify the bearer about which an inference is made, the additional premise, and the rules for interpreting evidence.
\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.35cm,style=nextline]
\item[$B_{\mathrm{id}}$] Specify the system boundary, a macrostate, and a group of admissible coordinate relabelings. The description is invariant under these relabelings. With external input held fixed, the state must influence its successor through an internal causal chain. A continuous lineage requires a unique successor. Symmetric cloning of a KV cache followed by independent continuation of both copies is recorded as a \texttt{BranchEvent}. The two causal descendants share an ancestor and receive distinct branch identifiers. This is a convention for tracking histories. The numerical identity of an experiencing subject remains open.
\item[$B_{\mathrm{nom}}$] Write $C=I_{\mathrm{cont}}\land C_{\mathrm{med}}\land A_{\mathrm{ind}}\land D_{\mathrm{dyn}}$. Here $I_{\mathrm{cont}}$ specifies continuity. $C_{\mathrm{med}}$ requires internal mediation with the positions of the earlier choice and signal blocked, together with transfer of the same rule to a different type of task. $A_{\mathrm{ind}}$ requires independent tests that several modules can access shared content and that an assessment of reliability can alter an action. $D_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ requires a lasting reorganization caused by processing that causally affects subsequent processing after the episode. We adopt the sufficient condition $\forall u\,[C(u)\Rightarrow E(u)]$ as a hypothesis.
\item[$B_{\mathrm{epist}}$] The status \texttt{UN} marks a question left open by the available evidence. The decision labels \texttt{NONE}, \texttt{INVESTIGATION\_PRIORITY}, and \texttt{SENTIENCE\_CANDIDATE} each require a separately stated basis. A numerical degree of belief requires priors and an observation model.
\end{description}
\begin{theorem}[Conditional theorem T6]
Assuming $B_{\mathrm{nom}}$, $C(u)$ entails $E(u)$. If there exists at least one operational model $M$ consistent with the evidence and $B_{\mathrm{id}}$ in which $C(u)$ is false, the same evidence remains consistent with both values of $E(u)$ after $B_{\mathrm{nom}}$ is added.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first claim applies the implication at $u$. For the second, put $S=\{v:C(v)\}$ in $M$ and expand the same operational model by the interpretations $E_0=S$ and $E_1=S\cup\{u\}$. Both contain $S$ and satisfy the bridge postulate. Since $u\notin S$, they disagree on $E(u)$. The two expansions have the same operational data and the same outcomes for every specified intervention.
\end{proof}
The absence of a measurement of a conjunct does not, by itself, prove that conjunct false. For Rule~110, a one-bit register, a lookup-table decoder, and frozen OLMo, the operational status of $E(a,t)$ in this work is \texttt{UN}. Transfer by an external token leaves internal mediation and independent architectural indicators unresolved. The causal abstraction framework of Geiger et al.~\cite{geiger2025} is applicable to the proposed test AI.04. Distributed Alignment Search (DAS) identifies a candidate subspace for the variable $p$. On independent examples, with the original positions $h,e$ blocked, one can substitute a donor history's value in this subspace and test whether the answer follows the donor's rule. A change in the KV cache within an episode is also insufficient to establish lasting plasticity.
